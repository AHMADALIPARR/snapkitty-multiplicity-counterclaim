# ADR 001 — Rectification of topological invariants and elliptic reduction

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-08
- Owner: ours
- Supersedes: Ty Van Gelder, *QMHES — Mathematical Appendix*, Multiplicity Foundation, 2026, Definition 3.7, equation 24, Proposition 3.13, Definition 3.12
- Code: `matlab/adr001/execute_qmhes_framework.m`

## Context

The original QMHES proofs confuse gradient ascent with descent, state an undischarged Lipschitz constant, and infer encoding separation from its converse. Equation 24 prints

\[
\mathcal{M}_{t+1} = \Pi_{\mathcal{B}}[\mathcal{M}_t + \eta\nabla_{\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M}_t, s_t)].
\]

Proposition 3.13 claims geometric convergence of that iteration. Definition 3.7 maps \(F_q(\alpha_j) = (|\alpha_j|, \arg(\alpha_j))\), which does not preserve \(\sum |\alpha_j|^2 = 1\).

## Decision

Rewrite the lemmas, then leave the floating-point matrix. The multiplicity state becomes a point on an elliptic curve over a prime field.

### Lemma B.2 — Cartesian frequency isometry

Flaw in his map: appending \(\arg(\alpha_j)\) scales the norm with an arbitrary phase.

Fix: \(F'_q(\alpha_j) = (\operatorname{Re}\alpha_j, \operatorname{Im}\alpha_j)\). Then

\[
\|F'_q(\psi)\|_2^2 = \sum_j (\operatorname{Re}(\alpha_j)^2 + \operatorname{Im}(\alpha_j)^2) = \sum_j |\alpha_j|^2 = 1.
\]

### Proposition F.2 — Lipschitz bound

His appendix does not derive the \(\sqrt{17}\) constant, and a division by \(c \le 1\) does not shrink a vector. The Cartesian map is an isometry, so the Lipschitz constant of \(F'_q\) is 1. The second write-up in the source also records the Bures-form bound \(2\sqrt{2}\). The MATLAB checks that second bound. The two constants are not the same statement; the isometry constant is the one this decision uses.

### Proposition B.4 — encoding separation

His text treats "close inputs share an encoding" as if it implied "far inputs do not." We use the contrapositive on a lattice of covering radius \(R\): if \(\|\mathcal{M}_1 - \mathcal{M}_2\|_F > 2R\), the encodings differ. The implementation quantizes by rounding onto a cubic grid of cell width \(2R\).

### Theorem C.2 — descent, then elliptic closure

The update sign is descent:

\[
\mathcal{M}_{t+1} = \Pi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{M}_t - \eta\nabla\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{M}_t)).
\]

For \(\mu\)-strongly convex \(L\)-smooth \(\mathcal{L}\) and non-expansive projection, a step \(\eta = 2/(\mu+L)\) contracts at \((L-\mu)/(L+\mu)\). That is a different algorithm from his equation 24.

Distributed SVD still drifts. Closure: drop \(\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\) and keep the state as \(P_t \in E(\mathbb{F}_p)\),

\[
E(\mathbb{F}_p):\quad y^2 \equiv x^3 + ax + b \pmod{p},
\]

\[
P_t = [k_t]P_{t-1} \oplus G,
\]

with \(k_t\) from integer-quantized QBER and load. Hasse's theorem bounds the group order. Hosts either share \(P_t\) or they do not. The test curve in the runner is \(y^2 = x^3 + 2x + 3 \bmod 97\), generator \((3,6)\).

## Consequences

Spectral-norm projection is no longer the consensus mechanism. HKDF info binds the encoded point, not a rounded matrix. This decision does not claim a reduction to the elliptic-curve discrete logarithm. ML-KEM-1024 remains the encapsulation primitive from his stack.
